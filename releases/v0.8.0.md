# Velvet v0.8.0 — AI DJ: a set from a sentence

Velvet 0.8.0 adds an AI DJ for listeners and the admin side that makes it
trustworthy: a model that has actually read your library, a page that shows
exactly what it sees and spends, and a mixing engine that behaves like a mix
DJ. Everything is **off by default** and works with Claude or with a model
running on your own machine.

## For listeners: tell the DJ what you want

Open **Now Playing → AI DJ** (once your admin has switched it on) and write
what you want to hear, in your own words:

- *"two hours of 80s italo and hi-nrg that slowly builds up, no Modern Talking"*
- *"the top 10 of the House Top 1000"* — exactly those positions, in chart order
- *"one album of Cerrone from around 1985"* — one album, in track order
- *"give me the Grandmix 1983 and 1986"* — two songs, one each
- *"the most played disco song"*, *"never played lossless disco"*, *"from my playlist Extra Long 12-inches"*
- *"create a mix of 3 songs which match tempo, key and genre"*

You get a set to play, add to the queue or save as a playlist, with the
DJ's reading of your request as chips, notes on what it did ("years
widened", "album: Cerrone — IX (1982)", "chart positions 1–10"), and the
numbers that matter to a DJ: the largest tempo step and how many
transitions are harmonic.

The model never picks a song. It reads your request against a description
of your library and writes a brief; Velvet chooses the songs from the
database. A set only ever holds songs from your library, and when something
is missing the page says so honestly — including when the song exists but
sits in a collection you have switched off.

## Mixed like a mix DJ

A set in DJ order comes from one mixable corridor and moves in small steps:
no two neighbours more than about 3 BPM apart, keys matched on the Camelot
wheel (same key, one step round the wheel, or the relative major/minor),
one artist never twice in a row, one song never twice. "Songs that match
each other" is the strict version: one tempo, one genre, different artists.

## For admins: a model that has read your library

- **Library notes.** Once, the model reads a digest of every folder — size,
  years, lengths, main artists, genres, a few file names — and writes what
  each folder *is*: "Disconet is the New York DJ remix service", "Grandmix
  is Ben Liebrand's yearly megamix on Dutch radio", "Bootleg holds
  imitations, not the originals". You read, edit and correct them; the DJ
  uses them on every request. Later passes only describe new folders.
- **The price before the pass.** Saving a key sends nothing. The settings
  card shows what one full pass costs on your library at the configured
  model and on the others, per 1,000 songs and per request, and a checkbox
  to run it. On a 141,000-song library with Claude Haiku: 4 cents for the
  genre proposals, 9 cents for 662 folder notes, a tenth of a cent per
  request afterwards.
- **What the AI sees and does.** Every call with its tokens, cost, duration
  and result, and the exact prompts of every task.
- **Genre families for Auto-DJ** in profiles, with a one-pass analysis of all
  tags and folders, undo, and an overview of what the active rules do to
  every song.
- **Your own model.** Any OpenAI-compatible server — Ollama, LM Studio,
  llama.cpp, vLLM, LocalAI, Jan — under *Advanced*, with a connection test.
  Nothing leaves your network.
- **House notes**, a per-user switch for the AI DJ, and a rule-based reader
  that keeps the page working when the model cannot be reached.

## Auto-DJ

- Auto-DJ keeps its direction when Last.fm does not know an artist: it
  stays close to the last list that worked instead of jumping anywhere.
- Genre continuity is scored against the session, not only the current
  song, so one off-genre pick no longer sets the course.
- Credits separated by ";" or "," get similar artists for each name.
