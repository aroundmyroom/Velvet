# Velvet v0.9.0 — Velvet Rooms: multi-room playback on Sendspin speakers

Velvet 0.9.0 plays your library on speakers around the house, in sync.

## Velvet Rooms

Velvet implements the Sendspin protocol and acts as a Sendspin server. It
finds Sendspin speakers on your network, you allow or pair them in the admin
page, group them into rooms, and pick a room in the player.

- **Rooms.** Every speaker starts as a room of its own. Combine speakers into
  one room and they play the same music, sample-accurately in sync. Move a
  speaker, rename or split a room — the music on the other speakers does not
  stop. A speaker that joins a playing room starts in sync at the current
  position.
- **A queue on the server.** Once a room plays, the queue lives on the
  server. Close the browser, switch off the laptop — the room keeps playing.
  Rooms and their queues survive a Velvet restart.
- **Gapless.** One continuous stream per speaker: tracks join without gaps.
  Seek, jump to a song, play next and queue edits never interrupt the song
  that plays.
- **The speaker's own controls work.** Play/pause, next, previous, volume,
  mute, repeat and shuffle on the speaker control the room's queue; *play* on
  an idle room resumes what it last played. Speakers with a display show the
  title, artist, album and artwork, and get a colour palette taken from the
  artwork.
- **Audio** goes out as FLAC, PCM (up to 24-bit) or Opus — whichever the
  speaker prefers; Opus only to a speaker that asks for it. ReplayGain is
  applied on the server with your Loudness Normalisation settings, and never
  clips.

## What works with it

Anything that implements the Sendspin protocol as a player, for example:

| Device / program | Notes |
|---|---|
| Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition | ESPHome device, found automatically |
| Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 | ESPHome device, found automatically |
| Other ESPHome speakers | With the Sendspin media player component |
| sendspin-cpp-cli, sendspin-python-cli | Reference players for Linux, Raspberry Pi, macOS and Windows |
| Sendspin for Windows | Connects to Velvet itself; pairs with a pairing token |

Which pairing method, buttons, display and audio formats a device has is up
to the device; the admin page shows what each one reports and only offers
what it supports.

## Allow or pair

Every connection is encrypted, paired or not. A new speaker plays nothing
until you decide:

- **Allow** — one click. Encrypted, not authenticated; fine for a home
  network you trust.
- **Pair** — encrypted *and* authenticated. With a six-digit code the speaker
  shows or reads out, an eight-digit code printed on the device, or a pairing
  token (Sendspin for Windows: *Show Pairing Token*). A token for a speaker
  that is off is kept and used when it next connects.

A speaker that was reset to factory settings shows *Not recognised* and plays
nothing until you pair it again — Velvet never falls back silently to
unpaired play. When Velvet is not playing on a speaker it releases it, so
another Sendspin server can use it; **Connect** takes it back.

## In the player

The output button gets a **Velvet Rooms** section next to Browser, Server
Speaker and Sonos. Pick a room and the browser hands over its queue — the
current song, its position, playing or paused — and becomes a remote for the
room: play/pause, next, previous, seek, volume (all speakers together, keeping
their balance), clicking a song, add, play next, remove, drag, clear, shuffle
queue, Flow and Undo all go to the room. Changes made on a speaker or in
another browser show up within a second or two. **Play in this browser** takes
it back at the same song and position.

Users with the Cast permission can edit rooms from the picker (the pencil
next to *Velvet Rooms*).

**Auto-DJ** keeps working: when the room starts its last song, the browser
picks the next one with all your Auto-DJ settings and adds it. Two open tabs
never add twice.

## Lights, line-in and Sonos

- **Lights and visualizers.** Light rings, LED strips and displays with the
  Sendspin visualizer role get loudness, a spectrum, peaks and beats, stamped
  on the music's clock so they flash on the beat. Beats use Velvet's BPM
  analysis as a guide when the song has one.
- **Line-in.** A Sendspin source device — a turntable preamp, an AUX input, a
  Bluetooth receiver — plays in any room, in sync. A source needs its own
  approval (*Allow as source* in the admin page); allowing a speaker to play
  never allows its input, and a microphone-type device must be paired first.
  Optionally the line-in starts by itself when the device reports a signal.
- **Sonos speakers can follow a room.** A Sonos does not speak Sendspin;
  Velvet sends it the room's audio as a live stream, early by an adjustable
  delay (default 2 s) so it lines up with its own buffer. One stream across
  track changes, seeks and pauses; room volume includes the Sonos.

## Turning it on

1. **Admin → Velvet Rooms** → switch it on. The status shows *Running*.
2. Speakers on the network appear under **Speakers**. Allow or pair them.
3. Group them under **Rooms** (or keep one room per speaker).
4. In the player, pick the room in the output picker.

No restart is needed. Who may play to rooms: admins and users with the
**Cast** permission. Config key: `sendspin` (`enabled`, `name`, `port`,
`bind`, `path`, `discovery`).

## Network and Docker

- Velvet and the speakers must be on the **same network segment**: discovery
  is mDNS, which does not cross VLANs or routers without an mDNS reflector.
- Open **TCP 8927** (speakers connect to Velvet) and **UDP 5353** (mDNS) on
  the Velvet host.
- **Docker:** mDNS does not reach a container on the default bridge network.
  Use `network_mode: host`, or publish port 8927 and point the speakers at
  `ws://<docker-host>:8927/sendspin` (or `<docker-host>:8927` in apps that ask
  for host and port).
- **IPv6:** discovery works over IPv6 too. For speakers to reach Velvet over
  IPv6, set the bind address to `::`.
- Velvet Rooms runs entirely on your network. Nothing is sent to any outside
  service and no account is needed.

## Backup

Backups now include the Velvet Rooms state: the server key and the pairings
(always together), rooms and known speakers. If the server key is lost — a
fresh install without a backup — every speaker has to be paired again.

## Current limits

- **Sonos follows, it is not sample-accurate.** It stays roughly in line with
  the Sendspin speakers; set the delay by ear.
- **Visualizer beats come from live analysis.** Songs without a clear pulse
  get few beats, and the first beat of each bar (downbeats) is not reported.
- **Auto-DJ tops up a room only while a browser tab with that room open is
  running.** Close the last one and the room plays to the end of its queue
  and stops.
- Radio and podcast streams stay in the browser. The equaliser and crossfade
  are browser-only and do not apply to rooms.

## For integrators

Admin routes under `/api/v1/admin/sendspin/*` (status, settings, allow,
revoke, rename, forget, connect, unpair, token and code pairing, line-in
sources) and player routes under `/api/v1/rooms*` (rooms, state, queue,
play, add, jump, remove, move, reorder, transport, seek, volume, mute,
repeat, shuffle, line-in, Sonos followers). Queue edits take a
`queueVersion` and answer `409 queue_changed` when the queue changed
meanwhile. The full contract — objects, limits, error codes, polling — is in
`docs/API/rooms.md`; the index is in `docs/API.md`. The listener's guide is
`docs/rooms.md`.

```bash
docker pull ghcr.io/aroundmyroom/velvet:v0.9.0
```
